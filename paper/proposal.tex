\begin{abstract}
We propose a probabilistic prediction system for the NCAA Men's Basketball Tournament (``March Madness''), targeting both the Kaggle \emph{March Machine Learning Mania} competition (2027 edition) and live trading on the Kalshi prediction market. Our approach combines three methodological innovations over standard baselines: (1)~a \textbf{Mixture-of-Experts} architecture that routes matchups to specialized sub-models by game type, (2)~a \textbf{Transformer sequence model} that encodes a team's full-season trajectory into a dense embedding, and (3)~a \textbf{market-calibrated ensemble} that fuses model predictions with betting market prices via Bayesian updating. We backtest on 12 years of tournament data (2014--2026, $\sim$756 games) using leave-one-tournament-out cross-validation, targeting a Brier score below 0.170 (vs.\ $\sim$0.200 seed baseline and $\sim$0.175 Vegas benchmark).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The NCAA Division~I Men's Basketball Tournament is a 68-team, single-elimination event producing 63 games over three weeks each March. Its high variance---driven by single-elimination format, large talent gaps, and emotional intensity---makes it a canonical testbed for probabilistic prediction.

The Kaggle \emph{March Machine Learning Mania} competition, running annually since 2014 with a \$50{,}000 prize pool, asks participants to submit $P(\text{Team}_i \text{ beats Team}_j)$ for every possible matchup, scored by Brier score:
\begin{equation}
    \text{BS} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(p_i - o_i)^2
    \label{eq:brier}
\end{equation}
where $p_i$ is the predicted probability and $o_i \in \{0,1\}$ is the outcome. Lower is better.

A key empirical finding from 12 years of competition: \textbf{simple models with strong features consistently outperform complex ML}. Most winners use logistic regression or Elo with KenPom efficiency ratings~\citep{pomeroy2004}. The notable exception is \citet{landgraf2017}, who won by modeling competitors' submissions (meta-prediction) rather than improving game-level prediction.

This project aims to push the prediction frontier through structured model heterogeneity (Mixture-of-Experts), temporal context (Transformer), and market integration (Kalshi), while respecting the fundamental constraint that 63 games per tournament provide very limited signal.

\subsection{Connection to Prior Work}

This project extends our prior NBA analytics research in \texttt{sports\_project/}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Context-Weighted RAPM} (CW-RAPM): 1.9\% RMSE improvement over standard RAPM via stint-level quality weighting~--- analogous context-weighting applies to college basketball ratings
    \item \textbf{Volatility regime detection}: Our 6-factor model with novel Volatility Factor and HMM regime switches transfers to modeling tournament vs.\ regular-season dynamics
    \item \textbf{Championship prediction}: AUC $\sim$0.78 on 20-year NBA data using bench depth, rotation entropy, and stagger index
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Feature Engineering}

We construct features in four layers of increasing sophistication:

\paragraph{Layer 1: Static Team Ratings.}
Pre-tournament snapshots of KenPom AdjEM, Barttorvik T-Rank, Massey composite rank, seed number, and conference strength (avg.\ conference AdjEM).

\paragraph{Layer 2: Matchup Features.}
For each pair $(A, B)$: seed difference $\Delta s$, rating differences $\Delta r_{\text{POM}}, \Delta r_{\text{SAG}}, \Delta r_{\text{MOR}}$, tempo mismatch $|T_A - T_B|$, offensive--defensive style clash ($O_A$ vs.\ $D_B$), geographic distance to game site, and coach tournament experience.

\paragraph{Layer 3: Temporal / Momentum.}
Rolling 10-game win percentage, average margin, late-season rating trend ($\Delta r$ over last 30 days), and conference tournament momentum.

\paragraph{Layer 4: Market.}
Market-implied probability from Kalshi/Vegas, model--market disagreement signal $|p_{\text{model}} - p_{\text{market}}|$.

\subsection{Model Architecture}

We employ a three-tier model hierarchy (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} conceptual).

\subsubsection{Tier 1: Baselines}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Seed Logistic}: $P(A \text{ wins}) = \sigma(\beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \Delta s)$ --- the simplest meaningful model
    \item \textbf{Elo Ratings}: Season-long Elo with tuned $K$-factor, home-court advantage, and inter-season regression
    \item \textbf{KenPom Logistic}: Logistic regression on AdjEM difference --- the standard strong baseline
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Tier 2: Advanced Models}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep, start=4]
    \item \textbf{Gradient Boosting} (XGBoost/LightGBM): Full feature set with walk-forward cross-validation. Expected to be the strongest single model based on competition history.

    \item \textbf{Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)}: The core methodological contribution. A gating network $g(\mathbf{x})$ classifies each matchup into $K$ game types, and specialized expert models $f_k(\mathbf{x})$ handle each:
    \begin{equation}
        P(A \text{ wins} \mid \mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} g_k(\mathbf{x}) \cdot f_k(\mathbf{x})
        \label{eq:moe}
    \end{equation}
    Trained via EM: alternate between soft cluster assignment and expert parameter updates. Hypothesized game types:
    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \emph{Chalk} (1--4 vs.\ 13--16): High-seed dominance, rare upsets
        \item \emph{Competitive} (5--8 vs.\ 9--12): Upset-prone, seed provides weak signal
        \item \emph{Toss-up} (close seeds): Near coin-flip, momentum/matchup matters
        \item \emph{Cinderella} (mid-major vs.\ power): Conference mismatch dynamics
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Transformer Sequence Model}: Encode each team's season as a sequence of game embeddings:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{h}_{\text{team}} = \text{TransformerEncoder}(\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{e}_T)[\texttt{CLS}]
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathbf{e}_t$ encodes game $t$'s opponent rating, margin, location, and box score. Matchup prediction via MLP on $[\mathbf{h}_A ; \mathbf{h}_B]$. Architecture: 4 layers, 64-dim, 4 heads. Pretrained on regular-season games, fine-tuned on tournament outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Tier 3: Meta-Models}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep, start=7]
    \item \textbf{Weighted Ensemble}: $\hat{p} = \sum_m w_m \hat{p}_m$, weights $\{w_m\}$ optimized on holdout Brier score via constrained optimization ($w_m \geq 0$, $\sum w_m = 1$).

    \item \textbf{Market-Calibrated Ensemble}: Bayesian update of ensemble prediction $\hat{p}$ with market-implied probability $p_{\text{mkt}}$:
    \begin{equation}
        p_{\text{final}} = \frac{\hat{p}^{\alpha} \cdot p_{\text{mkt}}^{1-\alpha}}{\hat{p}^{\alpha} \cdot p_{\text{mkt}}^{1-\alpha} + (1-\hat{p})^{\alpha} \cdot (1-p_{\text{mkt}})^{1-\alpha}}
    \end{equation}
    where $\alpha \in [0,1]$ controls trust in model vs.\ market (tuned on backtest).

    \item \textbf{Portfolio Optimizer} (Kaggle-specific): Generate $N$ bracket portfolios maximizing $P(\text{top-}K \text{ finish})$ given ensemble probabilities and estimated competitor distribution~\citep{landgraf2017}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Kalshi Trading Strategy}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Signal}: Trade when $|p_{\text{model}} - p_{\text{market}}| > \tau$ (threshold tuned on backtest)
    \item \textbf{Sizing}: Fractional Kelly criterion ($f = 0.25 \cdot f^*_{\text{Kelly}}$) for bankroll safety
    \item \textbf{Execution}: Limit orders via Kalshi REST API (0\% maker fee)
    \item \textbf{Risk}: Max 5\% bankroll per game, max 20\% total exposure
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Backtest Protocol}

Leave-one-tournament-out (LOTO) cross-validation on 12 tournaments (2014--2026). For each holdout season $s$:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item Train on all seasons $\neq s$ (regular season + tournament)
    \item Predict all tournament matchups in season $s$
    \item Compute Brier score on $\sim$63 games
\end{enumerate}
Report mean $\pm$ std Brier across 12 folds.
